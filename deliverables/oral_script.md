# Oral script (about 3 minutes)

*Main figure on screen throughout: `figures/main_frontier.png`.*

---

**[0:00] The answer first.**
We were asked to put $3M into this startup at a $25M pre-money valuation. Our answer is no, not at this price. The reason is one missing photon.

**[0:15] The game.**
The startup sells a coordination game. Two desks each get a signal and must answer without talking. With any fixed rule they win at most 75% of rounds. With shared entangled photons, the startup says it wins 82%. Over 1,000 trials, that is real. It is +5.1 standard errors above 75%.

**[0:40] The photon-loss finding.**
But photons fade in fibre. On the startup's own 40 km link, only 40% of photons reach each desk. Both arrive in only 16% of rounds. In a lab you throw the other rounds away. A trading desk cannot. It has to answer every round, and it cannot tell whether its partner's photon arrived. Now count every round. Under our unheralded fibre model, an 82% coincidence-level source would win about 64.1% of all attempted rounds on a 40 km link. The company's denominator and protocol remain unverified. That is below the free 75% rule. If the 1,000 trials counted only rounds where both photons were detected, about 6,310 rounds were attempted.

**[1:20] Why the headline alone proves nothing.**
We also built a classical cheater. No entanglement at all. It is just allowed to drop rounds. It scores exactly 82% on the rounds it keeps.

**[1:35] The map.**
Here is our main figure. Across the bottom, the distance between the desks. Up the side, the time allowed to decide. Grey means there is time to send one classical bit, and one bit wins 100%. New York to London, the first product: if the desks can wait more than about 29.5 ms, a bit arrives and wins; if they cannot, the photons do not arrive either. Red means no time for a bit, but too many photons lost to beat 75%. The startup's own 40 km link is red. So is NYSE–Nasdaq. Green is the small patch in the corner, below 19 µs. The modelled 82% unheralded strategy exceeds the classical baseline only below ~5.7 km, under idealized assumptions (8.2 km ideal; ~17.6 km with optimized partially entangled states).

**[2:15] The money.**
On the startup's own payoff, counting every round, each round loses $21.82. At their target volume that is a loss of about $78M a year. And their $50M target is 1.0% of the whole global latency-arbitrage pie.

**[2:35] The best rescue, and what reopens the deal.**
The founders' best answer is quantum memories with heralding. Our model agrees that fixes the loss penalty: the win rate never drops below 75%. But they have not claimed memories, and it does nothing for London. We would look again with raw round-by-round logs, a named trade, and a fair price. The team's hardware may suit certified randomness instead. That is unproven.

**[2:55] Close.**
Real physics. Wrong product. Do not invest at this valuation.
