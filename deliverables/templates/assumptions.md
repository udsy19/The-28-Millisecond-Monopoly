# Modelling assumptions

Each assumption, why it is reasonable, and whom it favours. "Startup" means the assumption makes the startup look better than reality probably would; "us" means it strengthens our case against investing.

| # | Assumption | Why it is reasonable | Favours |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | The game is standard CHSH with uniform, independent input bits | That is how the case file defines it | Neutral (E5 relaxes it) |
| A2 | Ideal quantum win rate is {{ideal_q}} (Tsirelson bound); the startup's source quality is {{p_claim}} when both photons arrive | Textbook physics; we read the {{p_claim}} as a coincidence-level rate; the company's denominator and protocol remain unverified | Startup (we credit them the full {{p_claim}}) |
| A3 | Source quality below ideal is modelled as white noise mixed into a perfect singlet | Standard, simple noise model; reproduces {{p_claim}} exactly | Neutral |
| A4 | Photons reach each desk independently with efficiency η; a desk whose photon is lost plays the fixed answer 0 | A trading desk must answer every round and cannot see its partner's detector | Us, slightly: smarter fallbacks exist, so we also report the best-case floor |
| A5 | Best case for any fallback is Eberhard's {{eberhard_floor}} efficiency floor ({{dmax_floor}} maximum separation) | Eberhard showed such tests can work down to {{eberhard_floor}} with specially tuned (non-maximally entangled) states and near-perfect hardware; we treat it as the floor | Startup (it is unreachable in practice) |
| A6 | Fibre loss {{fibre_loss}}; source at the midpoint so each photon travels half the distance | Standard telecom fibre; midpoint placement minimises the worst arm | Startup (lowest-loss geometry; see A19) |
| A7 | Perfect detectors by default; sensitivity at efficiencies {{det_levels}} | Real detectors are below perfect; at the middle level the green zone shrinks to {{dmax_82_det09}} | Startup |
| A8 | Three causal states for one classical bit: physically excluded below d/c (vacuum light), route-dependent up to a straight-line fibre route at {{fibre_index}}, arrives above it; New York–London uses half the Hibernia round-trip benchmark ({{hibernia_one_way}}) | Separates what physics forbids from what a particular route delivers; the Hibernia figure is a 2015 round-trip claim, not a measured one-way service | Startup (straight-line fibre is faster than any real cable, so the route-dependent band is as narrow as it can be) |
| A9 | The {{race_window}} window from Aquilina, Budish & O'Neill is shown only as the fastest documented decision timescale | It measures equity latency-arbitrage races, a different market from block orders; it does not establish demand for this product | Neutral |
| A10 | {{pair_rate}} pairs/s means pairs *generated* at the source; only the both-arrive share is usable | Literal reading of a source specification | Us (alternative reading: delivered pairs, {{hours_delivered}} instead of {{hours_generated}}; neither changes the decision) |
| A11 | Payoff ±{{payoff}} per round, costs {{cost_q}} and {{cost_c}} per round, as given | The case defines them; we flag them as invented | Startup (no real trade is shown to pay like this) |
| A12 | Heralded delivery (quantum memories) is a founder-rescue scenario only | Not claimed by the startup; memories at trading-desk scale are not commercial | Startup (we model it anyway) |

| A14 | Fixed random seed {{seed}}; {{n_mc}} Monte Carlo rounds per simulation | Reproducibility; Monte Carlo agrees with the formulas to within {{mc_err}} | Neutral |

| A16 | Fibre loss sensitivity: {{loss_017}} (Ding & Jiang) gives {{dmax_82_017}} at {{p_claim}} and {{dmax_floor_017}} best case; a hypothetical {{loss_010}} fibre gives {{dmax_82_010}} and {{dmax_floor_010}} | Shows how much better glass would buy | Neutral (reported, not assumed) |
