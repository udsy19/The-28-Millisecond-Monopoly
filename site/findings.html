Findings / Investment memo

The photon that didn't show up.

Recommendation: do not invest at a pre-money valuation. The physics is real, but under our model of the startup’s own setup (photons sent unheralded over fibre, a desk that must act on every round), its two headline numbers, quantum success and a fibre link, cannot both hold for a trading desk, and no one has named a trade that pays like the game being sold.

What is being sold, and what we tested

The product is a coordination game: two desks each get a yes/no signal and must answer without talking. Entanglement (linked photon pairs shared in advance) lets them coordinate more often than any classical (ordinary, non-quantum) rule. We rebuilt it in code and tested every case-file number.

  • The physics checks out. The best rule without communication wins of rounds; the ideal quantum strategy wins . The startup’s own classical demo () is a fair baseline, statistically consistent with .
  • But the desks already share a private line. If there is time to send one bit, a simple classical rule wins of rounds. Quantum only matters when the decision must be made faster than light can travel between the desks.

The finding: photons go missing, and a desk cannot skip rounds

A lab throws away rounds where a photon is lost. A trading desk must answer every round, and it cannot tell whether its partner’s photon arrived. On the startup’s own link only about of photons reach each desk, so both arrive in just of rounds. Counting every round, a source of the startup’s quality wins , which is worse than the free rule. To beat , each desk must receive of its photons, so the modelled strategy only wins below about under idealized assumptions (an ideal source: ; specially tuned states at Eberhard’s floor: ). Stored, pre-confirmed pairs (heralding) have no fixed distance cutoff, but are limited by pair supply and quantum memories the startup has not claimed.

So an rate on rounds where both photons were detected is consistent with this link; an rate over all attempted rounds is not. If the trials were both-photon rounds, about rounds were attempted; the startup’s denominator and protocol are unverified. A purely classical “cheater” that quietly discards some rounds scores exactly with no quantum hardware at all, so the headline proves nothing without the discard count.

The money does not add up either

On the startup’s own numbers the edge is per round, so a year needs rounds. Counting every round on the link, the edge is per round: a desk would switch the photons off and take the free , so the product is worth nothing there (used as sold, it would lose about a year). The ± payoff is invented by the case, and of profit would be of the ~ a year global equities latency-arbitrage market (traders profiting from being microseconds faster), a generous comparator for block orders. The edge also vanishes unless each desk’s signal fires between and of the time; no trade has been named.

Did our hypothesis survive?

Yes: the decision (pass) survived, but the reason changed. We expected the shared private line to settle it: one classical bit beats entanglement whenever there is time to send it, which rules out New York–London. That alone leaves the founders a retreat to faster, closer trades. Photon loss closes it, under our unheralded model: in the only window where the bit cannot arrive in time, the photons do not arrive either. The evidence does not show a usable, profitable service; it does not disprove every possible version of one.

Questions for the founders

  1. Over what link, and from how many attempted rounds, were the trials scored?
  2. What is the end-to-end efficiency per desk (fibre, optics and detectors)?
  3. Which real trade has this payoff, and how often is each signal seen?
  4. What does the protocol do when one photon is missing?
  5. Can you sell without licensing the earlier patent on coordinating non-communicating parties (Szegedy, Ding & Shi), i.e. do you have freedom to operate?

What would reopen the deal

  • Logs of every attempted round showing better than between desks too far apart for a bit to arrive in time.
  • Heralded delivery with quantum memories (stored photons plus a signal confirming both arrived), with pair supply shown for the actual route: not claimed, and no help over direct transatlantic fibre.
  • A named trade with signals inside the window and a payoff tied to real profit and loss, at a research-stage valuation.

A pivot worth testing

The hardware may suit markets where entanglement is the product: certified randomness (provably unpredictable random numbers, already shown on a quantum computer by Liu et al.) or device-independent key distribution (encryption keys secure even on untrusted hardware). These still need high photon efficiency, but they do not have to beat a classical bit or match a trade. A hypothesis to test, not a proven market.

Limitations

Our loss model is simple (a desk missing its photon plays a fixed answer); cleverer fallbacks lower the bar, but never past the best case we report (). Perfect detectors are generous to the startup. The race window comes from equity latency arbitrage, a different market from block orders; we use it only as the fastest documented decision time. We modelled fibre only, not satellites. The payoff and the claim are the case’s own and could not be checked against a real trade.
