05Verdict

05. Do not invest at .

The physics is real. Under our model of the startup's own setup (photons sent unheralded over fibre, a desk that must act on every round), its two headline numbers, and a link, cannot both hold, and no one has named a trade that pays like the game. Our hypothesis survived, but the reason changed: in the only window where the bit cannot arrive in time, the photons do not arrive either. The evidence does not show a usable, profitable service; it does not disprove every possible version of one.

What would reopen the deal

  • Logs of every attempted round showing better than between desks too far apart for a bit to arrive in time.
  • Heralded delivery with quantum memories, with pair supply shown for the actual route. Not claimed, and no help over direct transatlantic fibre.
  • A named trade with signals inside the window and a payoff tied to real profit and loss, at a research-stage valuation.

Questions for the founders

  1. Over what link, and from how many attempted rounds, were the trials scored?
  2. What is the end-to-end efficiency per desk?
  3. Which real trade has this payoff, and how often does each signal fire?
  4. What does the protocol do when one photon is missing?
  5. Do you have freedom to operate around US Patent 11676104B2?

A pivot worth testing

The hardware may suit markets where entanglement is the product: certified randomness or device-independent key distribution. Both still need high photon efficiency, but neither has to beat a classical bit or match a trade. A hypothesis to test, not a proven market.
